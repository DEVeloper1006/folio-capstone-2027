Executive Summary & Purpose:

Folio is a mathematics workspace for macOS and iPad that lets users solve problems
and produce submission-ready PDFs in one workflow. Students solve math faster by
hand than by typing, but submitting typed solutions means rewriting everything in
LaTeX. Folio removes that rewrite.

On macOS, users enter math through keyboard input with context-aware shortcuts
inspired by Vim and Emacs. The shortcuts change based on the current task, selected
work, and subject context. AI-powered shortcuts turn intended operations into
mathematical notation, so users type less and work faster. On the iPad, users write with
the Apple Pencil. The app converts handwriting into structured, editable typeset math.
Users correct recognition errors before accepting results. Both platforms share a
common document format, so work transfers between devices.

The core technical contribution is a pipeline that captures handwritten or typed
expressions, converts them into a structured internal representation (Math IR), and runs
symbolic manipulation through commands: `/rref`, `/matmul`, `/derivative`, `/integral`,
`/simplify`. Recognition and computation are separate. A verified structured expression
can be manipulated with deterministic correctness even if handwriting recognition made
mistakes upstream. On macOS, where input is structured, commands are high priority.
On iPad, commands follow once the recognition pipeline is reliable.

The first release targets linear algebra, calculus, and proofs. Features: mathematical
input and editing, shortcuts for duplicating and modifying previous steps, matrix
operations, selected mathematical transformations, AI-assisted step checking, local
persistence, and PDF export. AI helps users check steps, catch errors, and convert
intended operations into proper notation. Both apps preserve step order and
explanations when saving and exporting. Editing shortcuts work on unfinished
expressions so users keep solving without stopping to fix formatting.
Testing compares Folio against handwriting alone and handwriting followed by LaTeX
transcription. Metrics: completion time, input errors, export correctness, usability after a
set practice period, and submission readiness.

Constraints & Assumptions
The software will be designed for modern web browsers, including Chromium, Microsoft
Edge, Firefox, and Opera, as well as native macOS and iPadOS applications. Operating
under generally accepted consumer privacy and safety guidelines, the project will
leverage open-source libraries and software with appropriate attribution whenever
possible, with no reliance on proprietary third-party software. Additionally, it is assumed
that user hardware across all defined personas will meet the baseline performance
capabilities required to run the application efficiently.

Data Strategy & Quantitative Metrics

Folio uses public handwriting datasets, synthetic data, and internal benchmarks to
develop and evaluate its mathematical recognition and reasoning systems.

Data Sources:
The primary datasets include:
   ●​ MathWriting: Main handwriting dataset with over 230,000 real and 396,000
      synthetic mathematical expressions.
   ●​ CROHME 2023: Handwritten expressions with LaTeX and structural annotations
      for independent evaluation.
   ●​ HME100K: Optional dataset for greater handwriting variation.
   ●​ Internal Benchmark: Team-generated expressions covering algebra, calculus,
      matrices, fractions, and varying handwriting quality.

Synthetic expressions will supplement public datasets, while training and evaluation
data remain separate to ensure reliable comparisons.

Recognition and AI Models: Folio will compare specialized recognition models
(UniMERNet), fine-tuned vision-language models (Uni-MuMER/Qwen), general
multimodal models (Gemini), and a custom structure-first pipeline that converts
handwriting into Math IR. Models will be evaluated based on accuracy, structural
correctness, latency, computational requirements, and deployment costs.

Folio also plans to post-train open-source language models (8B-70B parameters) using
Modal or AWS for mathematical reasoning, step verification, hints, and explanations.
Training datasets include PRM800K, MATH, NuminaMath-CoT, OpenMathInstruct-2,
and internally generated data. A proposed reinforcement learning environment will
support mathematical proof verification across algebra, calculus, linear algebra, discrete
mathematics, combinatorics, probability, and statistics. These advanced AI features will
remain separate from Folio’s core recognition and Math IR functionality.

Evaluation Metrics:
System performance will be measured using:
   ●​ Recognition Accuracy: Exact expression matching (ExpRate) and canonical
      equivalence (CDM).
   ●​ Structural Accuracy: Correct representation and conversion of expressions into
      Math IR.
   ●​ Capture Success: Accurate identification of handwritten regions.
   ●​ Performance: Recognition latency, command execution time, and computational
      cost.

Risk Management & Feasibility Analysis
Folio is feasible for a five-person team over two semesters:
  ●​ A single TypeScript codebase serves the web, macOS and iPad apps.
  ●​ Matrix operations and substitution are computed exactly by our own engine, never
      by a language model.
  ●​ Every handwriting recognition result and every AI suggestion has to be accepted
      by the user before it changes their work.

The main risks are handwriting recognition accuracy, AI response speed (NFR-02), and
GPU availability. All three will be tested before the November 22 proof of concept.

                     Part III: Project Development & Execution Plan

Team Communication & Meeting Cadence
We will be meeting 2x weekly on Saturdays and Thursdays to discuss engineering,
product and vision. Other meetings are done on an ad-hoc basis but we will have these
two meetings every week for all hands. Our meeting cadence is documented in our
github repository

Engineering Workflow & Version Control
Folio uses GitHub for version control and pull-request reviews, and Linear for issue
tracking and sprint planning. A monorepo structure keeps the frontend, backend,
mathematical processing, machine learning, tests, and documentation together to
simplify collaboration and integration.

Repository Structure:
    folio/
      ↳src/
         ↳ frontend/ – SwiftUI interface (iPadOS), React Electron (Desktop)
         ↳ backend/ – APIs, persistence, and orchestration
         ↳ math/ – Math IR, parsing, commands, and equivalence
         ↳ ml/ – Preprocessing, inference, and evaluation
      ↳tests/ – Unit, integration, and end-to-end tests
      ↳benchmarks/ – Fixed evaluation benchmarks
      ↳datasets/ – Dataset manifests and processing scripts
      ↳docs/ – SRS and technical documentation
      ↳meetings/ – Team meeting records
      ↳scripts/ – Build and utility scripts
      ↳.github/workflows/ – CI configuration

Development occurs on short-lived branches linked to Linear issues, with main kept
stable. Pull requests require passing automated checks, relevant tests, and at least one
team member’s approval. GitHub Actions handles formatting, static analysis, builds, and
automated testing, while larger ML evaluations run separately.


The team follows approximately two-week sprints, prioritizing tasks with clear
acceptance criteria and reviewing progress and blockers at each cycle’s end. Tasks are
complete once acceptance criteria are met, tests pass, documentation is updated, and
changes are merged. ML tasks additionally require recorded configurations and
evaluation results.

Infrastructure & Compute Resources
    -​ Team Macs and at least two iPads with Apple Pencil. iPad builds and Apple
        signing require a Mac.
    -​ CAS GPUs, Modal and AWS are used to post-train open-source models from 8B
        to 70B parameters on public and synthetic data.
    -​ Personal 4060Ti (8GB) and 5070Ti (16GB)
    -​ The post-trained models run on Modal or AWS. Cloud servers shut down
        automatically when idle and have spending caps.
    -​ A cloud virtual server hosts the API. It handles requests and rate limiting and
        connects the apps to the model servers. It does not store user documents.
    -​ GitHub Actions runs the tests.
    -​ The web app is hosted on vercel. The iPad app is delivered through TestFlight.
        The macOS and iPad apps require an Apple Developer Program membership
        (USD $99 per year).
